Lesson 0017

Working a map

One ticket per session, resolved by conversation and recorded in three places — and then a hand-off you must not skip, because skipping it throws away everything the map was for.

Trigger: a map exists. You invoke /wayfinder <map>, optionally naming a ticket. Without one, "you pick the next decision, not the user".

The session rule

The exception is consistent: research tickets are AFK and resolved by subagents, so several can run in parallel without consuming the session's attention. Every other type is a conversation, and conversations do not share a window well.

The five steps

Load low-res, zoom on demand

Steps 1 and 3 are a context-budget strategy. The map is loaded whole because it is an index; individual ticket bodies are fetched only when that ticket turns out to matter. This is how a plan too big for one window gets worked from one window.

Claim before working

"assign it to yourself before any work" — on GitHub, gh issue edit <n> --add-assignee @me, described in the tracker template as "the session's first write". The reason is at the bottom of the skill: "The user may run unblocked tickets in parallel, so expect other sessions to be editing the tracker concurrently." The assignment is the lock.

Recording is three writes, not one

Step 4, unpacked.
WriteWhereWhy
The answer, in full A resolution comment on the ticket A decision lives in exactly one place
Closing The ticket A closed ticket is unambiguously off the frontier — and unblocks its dependents
A gist plus a link The map's Decisions so far The map is an index; it never restates the decision
Contrast this with lesson 15

/implement has no completion step and leaves closing to you. /wayfinder has closing written into step 4 as a named instruction.

Same tracker, same frontier mechanism, two skills — and only one of them closes the loop. Knowing which is which is the difference between a flow that advances and one that stalls.

Ruling out of scope is not resolving

When a ticket turns out to sit past the destination: "close it (a closed ticket is unambiguously off the frontier) and leave one line in the Out of scope section: the gist plus why it's out of scope, linking the closed ticket. It stays out of Decisions so far, which records the route actually walked — a scope boundary isn't a step on it."

The hand-off, and the mistake

Repeat until nothing is left to decide. Then:

Feed /to-spec the map issue, not the individual decision tickets — /to-spec #<map_issue>, per its own trigger table. The map's job was to point at things; the spec's job is to collapse what it points at into one document. Then lesson 14 onward.

Plan, don't do

One more instruction governs everything above, and it names its own failure mode with unusual precision:

A feeling reinterpreted as a signal

The urge to stop planning and start building is normally a distraction to resist. Here it is read as information: it usually means the fog has cleared and the map is finished. The right response is to hand off, not to push through.

Check yourself

A grilling ticket resolves and you still have most of the session left. What now?

"Never resolve more than one ticket per session — with the exception of research tickets." Graduating newly-specifiable fog is part of step 5 of this ticket's resolution, so that much happens; taking a second ticket does not. And building is the thing wayfinder exists not to do.

Check yourself

Why must the cleared map go through /to-spec rather than straight to /implement?

The map is an index — the decisions live scattered across closed tickets. /to-spec is what gathers them into a buildable document. "Looping the map straight into /implement skips that collapse and throws the linked detail away", and the CHANGELOG names it one of the two most common mistakes with this flow.

What this sets up

All five flows are now covered. What remains is what happens when a flow stalls mid-session on a question it cannot settle by talking — three detours, each with a different shape of answer: lesson 18.

Read next, from the source

skills/engineering/wayfinder/SKILL.md — the whole thing, about 130 lines, and the most carefully written document in the set. Read the Fog of war and Out of scope sections together; the distinction between them is the idea the skill is built on, and it does not survive summarising.

Your agent is your teacher. Worth asking once a map exists: "show me the map at low resolution, then tell me which ticket is first on the frontier and why." Watching it choose — rather than choosing for it — is how you find out whether the blocking edges were wired correctly.

On spacing

Before moving on, state the one-ticket rule and its single exception, and name the three writes that record a resolution. Then say what happens when the map clears — in the skill's own three words, which are it hands off.
