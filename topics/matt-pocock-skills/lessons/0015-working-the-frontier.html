Lesson 0015

Working the frontier

One ticket, one fresh window, one commit — repeated. Plus the single missing step that stops the frontier advancing, which is the most load-bearing gap in the whole set.

Trigger: tickets are published with their blocking edges. Everything from here is a loop, and the loop has a hole in it you have to fill by hand.

Clear between every ticket

This is the phase-boundary tree answering yes at question 2. It is only correct because /to-tickets made each slice self-contained — the property that makes clearing safe was built in the previous step.

One ticket per invocation, and no parallelism

Worktrees help, but not completely

The same page: "Git worktrees are the community workaround, and note that refs/stash is shared across worktrees too, so worktrees alone do not fix the stash case. If you want parallelism today, you are assembling it yourself."

This is one of the four places lesson 19 shows the superpowers set filling a gap — using-git-worktrees exists for exactly this.

The gap

/implement ends at the commit. Read its nine lines again and there is no step after "Commit your work to the current branch". The docs page states the consequence, and it is worth reading twice:

The sentence to keep

If nothing gets closed, nothing ever becomes unblocked.

The frontier is defined as tickets whose blockers are closed. An unclosed ticket is not a tidiness problem — it is a build that has silently stopped, with every remaining ticket still showing as blocked.

Three separate things are being left undone, and it is worth separating them:

What /implement leaves for you, per ticket.
UndoneConsequence if you forget
Closing the ticket The frontier never advances. Everything downstream stays blocked
Ticking the acceptance criteria The ticket's own definition of done goes unchecked
Acting on the review findings The review produced leads and nothing consumed them

Review per ticket, or at the end?

The skill declines to decide, and gives you the trade honestly:

Note why per-ticket review is sharper on the Spec axis specifically: one ticket is one spec, so the axis has a single unambiguous thing to check against. Reviewing eight tickets at once gives it eight, and the finding "this doesn't match the spec" gets vaguer.

Check yourself

Three tickets are built and committed, but the fourth still shows as blocked. Why?

The frontier is defined as tickets whose blockers are closed, and implement has no completion step — it ends at the commit and never touches the work item. Committed is not closed. On a dependency chain this is the failure that stops everything while looking like progress.

Check yourself

Why does the router say to /clear between tickets but not before /to-tickets?

Grilling, spec and split build on the same thinking, so clearing between them destroys a primary source — phase-boundary question 1. Each ticket, by construction, carries everything needed to build it, so the previous ticket's context is genuinely disposable — question 2. The property that makes clearing safe was created by /to-tickets.

What this sets up

That is the big-feature flow, complete. One flow remains, for work too foggy to write a spec about at all — where the output is decisions rather than deliverables: lesson 16.

Read next, from the source

docs/engineering/implement.md — read as a list of things a nine-line skill does not do. It is the best worked example in the set of why reading the SKILL.md alone is not enough, and why the docs pages exist despite being a lower authority.

Your agent is your teacher. Worth setting up once: ask it to give you a single command that lists your open tickets whose blockers are all closed — your frontier, as a query. On a real tracker that is one gh invocation, and having it to hand makes the closing discipline much easier to keep.

On spacing
